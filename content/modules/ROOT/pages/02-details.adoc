== Accessing your environment

=== Connecting to Red Hat Developer Hub

. Open your browser and navigate to the URL provided by your instructor: `{rhdh_url}`
. On the login page, enter your credentials and select **GitLab** as the sign-in mode
. You will be redirected to the GitLab authentication page
. Enter your username (`{user}`) and password (`{password}`)
. You land on the Red Hat Developer Hub home page

=== Your workspace structure

Each participant has an isolated workspace on the OpenShift cluster. Your resources are prefixed with your username `{user}`.

You will have access to:

* A dedicated OpenShift namespace for development (`{user}-dev`)
* A preprod namespace (`{user}-preprod`)
* A production namespace (`{user}-prod`)
* GitLab repositories under your account
* Dedicated Dev Spaces workspaces per service

== Pre-workshop access checklist

Before starting the modules, verify the following:

* [ ] **RHDH access confirmed** -- Successfully logged in to `{rhdh_url}`
* [ ] **GitLab credentials working** -- Login with `{user}` / `{password}`
* [ ] **RHDH home page displayed** -- Page shows available tools
* [ ] **Stable internet connection** -- No VPN blocking access to services

== Practical tips

TIP: Keep the Red Hat Developer Hub window open in a dedicated tab. You will return to it frequently to access components, CI pipelines and deployment topologies.

IMPORTANT: Each module uses a separate Dev Spaces workspace. To save cluster resources, stop your Dev Spaces workspace at the end of each module using the **"<>"** button at the bottom-left of the IDE.

WARNING: Do not share your credentials with other participants. Each workspace is isolated and resource conflicts could prevent your pipelines from running correctly.

NOTE: If a pipeline fails, don't panic! It is often intentional in this workshop (notably in the Villain module which deliberately introduces a CVE). Error messages are explicit and guide you toward the solution.
